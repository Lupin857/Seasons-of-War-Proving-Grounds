Oasis

Play full games against an AI that learns. Train it by letting it play itself; it keeps whatever strategies win, and every game feeds the card win rates.

Rules this simulation uses

  • Two players, first to 3, 4 or 5 points while leading (chosen when you start a game; each length has its own AI, card stats and ratings). Ties keep playing. Game stops after 20 rounds.
  • Each shop: $3 + income. Money carries over. Display of 5 units and 5 buildings; unbought cards go sideways and come back once. After that, they go face-up to the discard pile.
  • Turn order: random in round 1; afterward the loser of the last battle shops first (draw: whoever shopped second).
  • Per shop: 3 buys of any kind (units, buildings or gear). Once per shop you may make one extra buy for $1 more. You may buy at most 1 Field per shop. Carpenter's Guild adds extra buys of any kind and makes buildings (not gear) $1 cheaper (minimum $1). War College lets you take duplicates of units you own from the discard pile for free, without using a buy (once per shop).
  • Selling: on your shop turn you may sell any number of cards for $1 each ($2 with The Bazaar), including cards you just bought this shop and gear in your hand. Selling doesn't use a buy or end your turn. A sold unit or building drops one level. A sold card goes face-up to the discard pile, so both players see which card it was (Fields return to the supply, and a level from Mason's Guild puts the Guild in the discard pile). Altar of Blood can't be sold.
  • Discard pile: a returning card that goes unbought again, a card someone sells, and a unit Altar of Blood takes are laid face-up in the discard pile. Only War College takes from it: free duplicates of units you own, without using a buy. Black Market cards you don't buy still go to the bottom of the deck.
  • Fields ($1, +$1 income) and Recruits ($1, 1-power token) are always for sale. City holds 5 buildings; duplicates share a slot, Fields don't.
  • Mobilize: up to 5 unique units (+Reserve Encampment); Recruits don't count. Smithy Armaments, Armory Shields, Study Training and Barracks Recruits are placed here, and gear from your hand is equipped: one gear per unit (Recruits too). A unit with a Zweihander can't hold Armaments or Shields. Gear only counts as equipped in battle; after the battle it all comes back to your hand, and the page remembers who wore what for next time.
  • Recruits are discarded after the battle phase.
  • Hidden information: the AI sees what is public (purchases, cities, money, the order you used last battle), never your current deck order.
  • Battle: same engine as the Battle Sandbox (Charge before Reveal; Vanguard reveals first, deaths after abilities, damage clears when hidden). While a Pigeon Hunter is charging or revealed, the opponent's units are Silenced: they have no ability text (their gear still works). Lethal damage kills whatever it hits (unless a Shield blocks it or it is reduced to 0). Gear on a dying unit stays attached to it in the graveyard, except Heirloom Blade, which moves to your Rearguard if the Rearguard is alive and has no gear. Both players' abilities in one round resolve together: tokens land first, then damage, so neither seat goes first.
  • Gear (in the building deck): War Banner, Healing Balm, War Horn, Zweihander, Battle Axe, Poison Oil, Lance, Heirloom Blade and War Drums. Bought like any card, it goes to your hand.
  • Altar of Blood: +$4 income, but at the start of each shop you discard one copy of your most expensive unit (you choose among ties).
  • Cathedral: +1 point while owned, but you can only win at the end of a battle phase in which you won the battle.
  • Mason's Guild attaches to one of your buildings and counts as an extra copy of it. Black Market ($2) sets aside the top 3/4/5 cards of the unit deck or the building deck (your choice, not both) once per shop; you may buy one of them for $1/$2/$3 less (by its level), using a normal buy. Unbought cards go to the bottom.
  • Miracles (optional, set at the start of a game): 14 in the unit deck and 8 in the building deck. A miracle is never shown alone: the next card from its deck is laid on top of it (more miracles stack). That card costs its price plus each miracle's surcharge (discounts apply to the total), and its buyer gets every miracle under it. Money miracles pay at once, and Discard Writ is used at once (up to 1 other display card goes to the discard pile and is replaced from its deck, then you take another shop turn before your opponent); the others go to your hand, which both players can see, and are played later: Divine Favor, Vision and Patronage on your shop turn (from the ✶ Miracles tab; a played Patronage takes $2 off your next buy), and Recruit, Armament and Training miracles at a Mobilize (from the Miracles panel: their Recruits or tokens go on your bench, and the miracle is discarded once you lock in with them placed). Unbought, a covered card goes sideways and leaves with its miracles. Black Market and Vision put a miracle they turn up on the bottom and look at one more card. Miracles can't be sold.
  • Automatic shop refills (optional, set at the start of a game): when you buy a card from the shop display, a new card from the same deck is dealt into its spot right away, so the display stays full all round.
  • Battle miracles (optional, a second checkbox): Ambush, Second Wind and Silence are shuffled into the unit deck, Divine Shield and Sabotage into the building deck, and Mill and Restock leave to make room. A battle miracle goes to your hand. At a Mobilize you may place it anywhere in your deck; it isn't a unit, so your Vanguard and Rearguard are still your first and last units. It resolves the moment it becomes the top card of your deck (one placed ahead of your Vanguard at battle start, one behind your last unit when that unit charges), together with that step's Charge abilities and the other player's battle miracles; the card under it is revealed only after. Back-to-back battle miracles resolve one after another. "Unit ahead" means the charging unit, or the Vanguard at battle start. While Pigeon Hunter is revealed, the opponent's battle miracles don't trigger. Used or not, placed battle miracles are discarded after the battle.
  • Discard pile and miracles: every miracle that is used up or discarded is laid face up in the discard pile, where it can't be bought.
  • More miracles: Covenant deals one more card from the same deck on top of it when it is dealt, and the two are bought together for their combined price (one buy; two buildings need two City slots). Mill, when used, sends the top 3 units of the unit deck to the discard pile. Restock, when used, deals a card from the same deck into the display. Freeze goes to your hand: on your shop turn, play it as your turn to set aside a display card with its miracles; its spot stays empty, and only you can buy it, at its normal price with a normal buy, on any later shop turn. Upheaval goes to your hand: on your shop turn, discard it (without using your turn) to send every display card to the discard pile, then deal new cards until the display is full.
  • Not in this version (0 copies on the sheet): Desperate Infantry, Veteran Knight, Mercenary, Arsonist, Chapel, Recruitment Office and Outpost. The War Drums battle miracle is retired (War Drums is now gear).
